Known supported variables

This device is known to support the following variables (values are just examples):

battery.charge

100.00

Only meaningful while on battery, where it tracks the battery voltage (about 100 down to 82 over most of a discharge, then a fast drop to 0 in the last three minutes). On mains it is a recharge ramp that restarts near 20 after any battery use and climbs back to 100, so a low value with ups.status OL does not mean a weak battery. The vendor software shows the same number. The driver only compares it with battery.charge.low while on battery.

Known supported commands

This device is known to support the following NUT’s instant commands:

  • beeper.toggle

    verified

  • shutdown.reboot

    verified, output off after ups.delay.reboot, back by itself about 15 s later

  • shutdown.return

    verified with and without mains; without mains the UPS powers itself off about a minute after the cut and returns a few seconds after mains is back

  • shutdown.stop

    verified, cancels a pending shutdown

  • test.battery.start

    verified, takes an optional duration in seconds

  • test.battery.start.deep

    verified, runs until the UPS reports low battery (about 20 minutes here, with the extended battery bank)

    verified, 10 seconds

  • test.battery.stop

    verified

About this device

SMS (Brazil) Premium 1500 VA, model PRM1500BiBiW, line-interactive, 115 V output (range string EBiS115), 24 V battery bus. It has a USB port with a built-in Prolific PL2303 serial adapter (067b:23a3), so it appears as a /dev/ttyUSB* node and is driven by sms_ser. The unit reported here has extra external batteries attached, so the run times below are longer than a stock unit would give.

this report is not from a stock NUT release. The sms_ser driver in NUT 2.8.1 (Debian 13 package 2.8.1-5) was rebuilt with the changes of NUT PR #3701 (issue #1987) backported; it reports itself as driver.version.internal 1.00+premium6. With the stock 2.8.1 driver the same unit reports an outage as "BYPASS" instead of "OB", never reopens the serial port after a USB replug, and its shutdown delays do not work (see the PR). The variables below are what the patched driver publishes; the stock driver publishes the same set minus ups.mfr, ups.model, ups.type, output.voltage.nominal, output.frequency.nominal, the ups.delay.* variables and battery.charge.low.

Measured on this unit: the UPS counts its test and shutdown delays in units of 0.6 s, and the restore delay of the shutdown command in minutes; the driver in the PR converts seconds accordingly. The UPS raises its own low-battery flag about two minutes before it cuts the output. A full discharge from 100 % to cut-off took about 25 minutes with a 12 % load and the extended battery bank.
